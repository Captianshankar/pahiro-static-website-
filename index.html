Ranked by combining slope-risk level with population exposure. Demonstration logic only.

  1. Burtuk (Ward 1) — very high slope risk with dense settlement on the upper slope; recommend slope inspection and evacuation readiness.
  2. Syari–Tathangchen (Ward 15) — long-running slow-moving slide below the old Palace grounds; recommend continued displacement monitoring.
  3. Upper Sichey (Ward 2) — fractured hillside with repeated creep; recommend drainage checks before peak monsoon.
  4. Ranipool (Ward 14) — loose fill slopes along the highway corridor; recommend traffic advisory during heavy rain.
  5. Chandmari slide belt — long-settled residential ward with a decades-long history of movement; recommend structural inspection of affected buildings.

About this platform

Pahiro (a regional word for landslide) is a concept demonstration of a real-time monitoring and early-warning platform for landslide-prone towns in the North Eastern Region. It combines a GIS view of population and risk, live rainfall and soil-moisture readings, a citizen and field-official reporting channel, and dashboards intended for district administrations and disaster management authorities.

Gangtok's hill slopes have a long record of instability, worsened by steep terrain, heavy monsoon rainfall and unplanned hill-cutting. A severe rainfall-triggered event in June 1997 caused widespread damage and loss of life across several wards, and smaller slides recur most monsoons, particularly around Chandmari, Sichey, Burtuk and Syari–Tathangchen.

What is real in this demonstration: the rainfall, forecast and soil-moisture readings are live figures for Gangtok's coordinates, drawn from a public weather data service. What is illustrative: ward boundaries, exact marker positions, road status, priority ordering and the risk-score thresholds are simplified for demonstration and are not a substitute for an official hazard assessment.

This is an independent concept prototype built to illustrate the idea above. It is not an official government service and should not be used to make evacuation or emergency decisions. In an emergency in Sikkim, contact the State/District Disaster Management Authority or dial 112.

Data sources: rainfall, forecast and soil moisture from the Open-Meteo public weather API; base map tiles from OpenStreetMap contributors; slope-risk and ward context adapted from published hazard studies of Gangtok. Built as a demonstration for the North Eastern Region landslide early-warning problem statement.
